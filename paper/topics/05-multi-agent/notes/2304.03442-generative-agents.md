# Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior

<https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442>

Park, O'Brien, Cai, Morris, Liang, Bernstein. Stanford University, Google
Research, Google DeepMind. UIST 2023.

## Core ideas

A sandbox town populated by agents that wake up, work, form opinions,
notice each other, and remember. The stated goal is **believability**,
not task completion, and that framing explains every design decision.

The architecture is three components, and it is a **single-agent** memory
design that happens to be instantiated many times.

- **Memory stream.** A complete record of the agent's experience in
  natural language, appended to and never summarized away. Retrieval
  scores each record on three axes and combines them: **relevance,
  recency, and importance**. Recency alone surfaces the trivial and
  fresh; relevance alone surfaces the on-topic and stale; importance is
  the agent's own rating of how significant the memory was.
- **Reflection.** A second kind of memory, synthesized from
  observations rather than recorded from the world. The agent
  periodically asks what its recent experience implies and writes the
  conclusion back into the stream, where it can be retrieved like
  anything else and can itself feed later reflections, forming a tree.
- **Planning.** High-level plans derived from those conclusions and the
  current situation, then recursively decomposed into concrete
  behaviours. Plans go back into the stream too.

The loop is closed: reflections and plans become memories, which shape
later retrieval, which shapes later reflection.

**The reflection example is the argument.** Asked who he would spend an
hour with, an agent with only raw observations picks whoever he has
bumped into most often, which is the wrong answer. With reflection, he
has already concluded that he is dedicated to his research and that
someone else is too, and picks that person instead. Frequency of
observation is not significance, and only a synthesis step can tell them
apart.

The ablation is the load-bearing evidence: observation, planning, and
reflection each contribute critically to believability, so none of the
three is decoration.

The multi-agent result is **emergent rather than engineered**. Telling
one agent she wants to throw a party produces invitations spreading,
acquaintances forming, and attendees coordinating to show up — none of
it scripted. The point is that no coordination protocol was written.

## What problem it solves

Simulating a population of believable characters previously meant
scripting each one, which does not scale and produces behaviour that is
consistent only where an author thought to make it so.

Underneath, the technical problem is that a long-lived agent accumulates
more experience than fits in a window, and that naive retrieval over that
experience produces shallow behaviour. Retrieval by relevance alone
cannot distinguish a formative event from a passing one, and no amount of
raw recall produces a conclusion.

## Limitations

- **Embellishment rather than fabrication.** The paper's own
  characterization is precise and worth keeping: agents rarely invented
  experiences wholesale and would admit not remembering, but they did
  decorate what they knew, adding a plan that was never discussed. They
  also leaked the base model's world knowledge into the fiction,
  attributing a famous book to a townsperson who happened to share a
  name.
- **Believability is the metric**, and it is evaluated by human judgment.
  Nothing here measures whether an agent achieved anything.
- The memory stream grows without bound, and retrieval over it grows with
  it.
- A small population over a short simulated period.

## What this means in practice

1. **The transferable contribution is a memory design, not a multi-agent
   design.** Relevance plus recency plus importance is directly usable in
   any long-running single agent, and it is a better default than
   similarity alone. Similarity search will happily return five near-
   duplicate trivia and drop the one decision that mattered.

2. **Score importance at write time, not at read time.** The agent rates
   a memory when it records it. That is cheap, it happens once, and it is
   what lets retrieval later distinguish a load-bearing event from
   background noise. Deciding significance at query time means paying for
   it on every query and doing it without the context in which the event
   happened.

3. **Reflection is a write, and it is what makes accumulated memory
   useful.** Raw history answers questions about what happened.
   Conclusions answer questions about what it means, and no retrieval
   strategy over raw records will synthesize one. If your agent
   accumulates history and still seems shallow, the missing piece is a
   step that periodically reads and concludes.

4. **Feed conclusions back into the same store.** Reflections being
   retrievable like observations, and being able to build on each other,
   is what makes the structure compound rather than flatten. A separate
   summary field that nothing reads back is not the same design.

5. **Expect embellishment specifically, and design against it.** The
   observed failure was not invention but decoration: a true fact plus a
   plausible addition. This is harder to catch than a fabrication because
   most of the sentence checks out. Where it matters, keep the record and
   the inference distinguishable, so a claim can be traced to an
   observation or identified as a conclusion.

6. **Emergence is a finding, not a plan.** The party spreading through
   the town is a genuine result and a bad model for building a product.
   If your system needs a thing to happen reliably, write the
   coordination; do not populate a simulation and hope.

## What it leads to next

- **Voyager (2305.16291)** carries the accumulate-and-reuse idea into an
  environment where what accumulates is executable skills rather than
  recollections, and where the payoff is measurable capability rather
  than believability.

Read against **2310.08560**, which solves the same overflowing-memory
problem as an operating system with explicit tiers and paging, where this
solves it as a scored retrieval over an append-only log. Read against
**2303.11366**, whose reflection is the same operation aimed at a
verdict rather than at a self-concept.

## Connection to the lab

`papers.yaml` maps this to two labs, and the pairing says something about
the paper: one is about memory and one is about running several agents,
and the first is where the substance is.

**`labs/track-2-tools-context/11-context-memory` is the real match.**
That lab's three tiers, its compaction step, and its scratchpad are the
engineering answer to the same problem the memory stream faces. The
sharpest connection is the lab's rule to **cut by value, not by age**,
which is this paper's importance score arriving from the other direction:
both are refusals to let recency alone decide what survives.

The lab's compaction and this paper's reflection are different operations
worth keeping distinct. Compaction is lossy compression to fit a budget;
reflection is inference that adds something not previously present. A
digest replaces turns, a reflection is a new memory. A system doing only
the first gets smaller; only the second gets deeper and still overflows.

**`labs/track-3-autonomy/13-subagents` is the weaker mapping, and useful
precisely for that.** That lab is blunt that delegation is not free: each
child re-establishes its own context, produces a report, and the parent
reads it, so for work the parent could finish in a few tool calls the
overhead exceeds the benefit. It also names the shape that does pay,
fan-out over genuinely independent work, and the shape that does not,
sequential work with a shared thread of reasoning.

This paper's population meets neither criterion, because the agents are
the subject of the experiment rather than a decomposition of a task. That
is the honest reading: it demonstrates what many agents *do*, not when
you should build with many.
